The PediEAT assessment evaluates feeding and swallowing abilities across four key domains: Physiology,
Processing, Behavior, and Selectivity. This comprehensive assessment provides insight into feeding safety,
efficiency, and participation.

Physiology domain assessment revealed deficits in oral motor coordination and increased risk for
swallowing safety concerns. Several instances of coughing and throat clearing were observed, indicating
possible penetration or aspiration during feeding. These findings suggest the need for vigilance in
monitoring swallowing safety and respiratory status during meals.

Processing domain evaluation demonstrated limited texture acceptance, particularly with mixed textures,
and a preference for smooth and predictable consistencies. The child's sensory processing of foods is
characterized by aversions to certain textures, impacting the ability to participate in diverse dietary
experiences.

Behavior domain analysis indicated frequent negative mealtime behaviors, including refusal and
distractibility, which contribute to prolonged feeding times. These patterns suggest the need for targeted
behavioral interventions to enhance engagement and compliance during meals.

Selectivity assessment revealed a preference for a limited variety of foods, particularly those with bland
flavors and uniform textures. This degree of food selectivity presents challenges for achieving a balanced
diet and meeting nutritional requirements, pointing to a need for strategic dietary expansion.
• Feeding therapy services to address identified areas of need
• Texture modifications and feeding progression strategies
• Oral motor exercises and feeding skill development
• Mealtime behavioral strategies and environmental modifications
• Caregiver education on safe feeding practices
• Nutritional monitoring and consultation as needed
Based on the PediEAT findings, feeding safety considerations include heightened awareness of aspiration
risk and the possible need for modified diet consistencies. Regular reassessment of feeding skills and
safety is recommended to ensure optimal nutrition and prevent feeding-related complications.
