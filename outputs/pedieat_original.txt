Physical Examination
• Body: Sabrina was seated upright with external trunk support. He demonstrated mild
leaning and occasional avoidance posturing during food presentation, suggestive of
postural instability and anticipatory defensiveness related to feeding.
• Head & Neck: Sabrina maintained midline positioning intermittently but frequently
turned his head away during non-preferred stimuli, indicating sensory-based aversion or
discomfort.
• Face: Facial features appeared symmetrical with a neutral affect throughout the session.
Mild visual distress (e.g., furrowed brows, gaze aversion) was observed during oral input.
• Jaw: No rotary or vertical jaw movements were present during oral trials. Sabrina did not
attempt to bite or chew when presented with soft solids. Repetitive jaw excursion accompanied by a frequent sucking noise was noted during feeding, consistent with
compensatory strategies to overcome poor intraoral pressure and ineffective lip seal.
• Lips: Lips were partially open at rest. His upper lip consistently inverted around the
bottle nipple. He demonstrated lip closure when feeding from a spoon.
• Tongue: No active tongue protrusion, lateralization, or elevation was observed. The
tongue remained in a neutral, retracted position without engagement in bolus
manipulation or oral transport.
• Cheeks: Bilateral hypotonia of the cheeks was noted, with no visible cheek activation
during sucking or spoon feeding. This lack of buccal stability likely contributes to
compromised bolus control and inefficient sucking.
• Palate: Hard and soft palates were structurally intact. However, multiple episodes of
coughing occurred during mealtimes, particularly with purees, raising concern for
delayed swallow initiation and potential airway compromise. No signs of nasal
regurgitation were noted.

Cranial Nerve Screening
• CN I (Olfactory): Not formally assessed. No significant response to olfactory stimuli or
food-related odors was observed during feeding.
• CN V (Trigeminal): Voluntary and reflexive jaw activity was absent. Sabrina displayed
decreased sensory tolerance to intraoral and peri-oral touch, when oral care tools or
feeding instruments approached or contacted the peri-oral area.
• CN VII (Facial): Facial symmetry was preserved at rest. However, minimal expressive
movement was observed during feeding. Lip closure remained weak, and cheek
activation was absent.
• CN IX (Glossopharyngeal): Gag reflex was not elicited. Swallow initiation appeared
impaired, with food remaining in the oral cavity and contributing to recurrent coughing
during and after oral trials.
• CN X (Vagus): Sabrina did not produce any spontaneous vocalizations during the
session. Despite the absence of vocal output, coughing was frequent during feeding trials,
though no throat clearing or changes in vocal quality were noted following episodes.
Voice remained clear post-meal, with no signs of laryngeal distress.
• CN XI (Accessory): Head positioning was upright with mild support. No impairments in
head or shoulder control were noted.
• CN XII (Hypoglossal): There was no evidence of tongue movement, including
protrusion, lateralization, or elevation. Absence of tongue activity during feeding
suggests limited function of the intrinsic and extrinsic tongue musculature, impeding
effective oral-phase skills.

Intraoral inspection revealed a visibly restrictive maxillary labial frenulum characterized by a
low insertion point on the gingival margin and tight tethering toward the interdental papilla
between the upper central incisors. The frenulum exhibited limited elasticity, with reduced
elevation of the upper lip upon manual retraction and observable blanching at the attachment
site, features consistent with a suspected Class II/III restriction based on visual screening,
warranting further evaluation by a specialist familiar with Kotlow classification or equivalent
anatomical grading. This anatomical presentation may be limiting functional labial mobility,
which is essential for achieving a strong anterior lip seal, maintaining oral containment of food
and liquids, and generating negative intraoral pressure for efficient sucking. During the
evaluation, he demonstrated compensatory oral motor strategies including increased jaw
movement and anterior spillage, suggesting that the tethered oral tissue may be contributing to
oral phase feeding challenges. These findings warrant further investigation in collaboration with
a pediatric dentist or ENT familiar with tethered oral tissues, particularly if ongoing feeding
inefficiencies, oral fatigue, or expressive speech concerns persist.

Sabrina presents with global feeding concerns supported by elevated scores across all subscales
of the Pediatric Eating Assessment Tool (PediEAT), with a total score of 194, indicating High
Concern across physiological, behavioral, sensory, and oral-motor domains.

Sabrina demonstrates notable difficulty coordinating respiration and feeding. He frequently
pauses after only a few sips when drinking from a bottle to catch his breath, suggesting immature
oropharyngeal coordination and possible respiratory fatigue. During bottle feeding, he exhibits
labial inversion—his upper lip tucking inward— rather than forming a proper outward seal or lip
flaring needed to maintain negative pressure

Behaviorally, Sabrina displays high distress during feeding, including food refusal, irritability,
and inconsistent acceptance of feeding routines. He often stops eating after a few bites and
demonstrates escalating stress as mealtime progresses. These behaviors are likely compounded
by underlying oral discomfort, fatigue, and poor feeding endurance, contributing to a negative
association with feeding activities.

Sabrina has a limited acceptance of textures, temperatures, and food types. He exhibits strong
preferences for familiar sensory experiences and shows distress when presented with novel or
mixed textures. Caregiver report and clinical observation confirm heightened oral sensitivity,
including gagging with certain foods and strong reactions to specific sensory input, indicating
significant sensory defensiveness. This rigidity may further impact the progression of his feeding
milestones, such as transitioning to age-appropriate solids and self-feeding.

Oral-motor observation revealed inefficient bolus formation and oral coordination. Sabrina
demonstrates behaviors such as over-chewing, food pocketing, and difficulty with tongue
mobility—all of which align with poor intraoral motor planning. During feeding, he relies
heavily on jaw movement, further confirming inefficient labial and lingual function. His inability
to maintain a proper lip seal and need for frequent breaks when drinking support the concern that
foundational oral-motor skills are not yet in place for safe, effective feeding. The presence of an
upper lip tie appears to contribute directly to these deficits by limiting labial mobility and suction
mechanics.
