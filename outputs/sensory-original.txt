Sabrina’s sensory processing abilities were assessed using the Toddler Sensory ProfileTM 2,
completed by his caregiver. Results revealed a multifaceted sensory profile with notable
variability across sensory domains. While Sabrina demonstrated typical responses to visual and
movement input, he exhibited significantly heightened sensitivity in tactile, oral, and behavioral
domains. His profile reflects both increased sensitivity and avoidance of specific types of sensory
input (particularly tactile and oral stimuli), contributing to challenges in regulation, participation,
and emotional responses in daily routines.
In the Touch Processing domain, Sabrina scored in the “Much More Than Others” range,
indicating a strong reaction to tactile input. His caregiver reported that he becomes upset when
having his nails trimmed, resists being cuddled, and avoids contact with cold, rough, or sticky
surfaces. He also demonstrates discomfort with temperature changes and clothing textures. These
findings suggest tactile defensiveness, which may impact his ability to engage in self-care
routines, exploration, and play. Gentle, graded exposure to tactile input and consistent routines
may support desensitization and comfort with touch-based activities.
In the Oral Sensory Processing domain, Sabrina also scored in the “Much More Than Others”
range. He demonstrates strong food preferences, difficulty transitioning to textured or chunky
foods, and frequently gags or holds food in his cheeks before swallowing. These patterns suggest
significant oral sensitivity, which contributes to feeding challenges. A structured sensory feeding
approach, including oral-motor play and gradual food exploration, may help improve his
tolerance and participation during mealtimes.
Sabrina also exhibited elevated scores in the Auditory Processing domain, placing in the “More
Than Others” range. He startles easily in response to loud or sudden sounds and becomes
distressed in noisy environments. He may also need more time to respond to auditory cues like
his name. These findings indicate that auditory input may be overwhelming and interfere with
engagement or attention. Modifying the auditory environment and providing visual support may
help him remain calm and focused during daily tasks.
In contrast, Sabrina demonstrated typical responses in the Visual and Movement Processing
domains. His responses in these areas fell within the “Just Like the Majority of Others” range,
suggesting that he tolerates and engages with visual and movement-based input well. He enjoys
looking at lights, spinning objects, and engaging in rhythmic activities like swinging or
bouncing. These sensory preferences can be used as strengths to support engagement and
regulation in therapy or home routines.

Sabrina’s Behavioral Responses associated with sensory processing fell in the “Much More Than
Others” range. His caregiver reported frequent tantrums, clinginess in unfamiliar settings, and
difficulty calming once upset. These behaviors are consistent with his sensory profile and
indicate that sensory sensitivities may be contributing to emotional dysregulation, particularly in
unpredictable or overstimulating environments.
Overall, Sabrina’s sensory profile reveals a complex interplay of heightened sensitivity and
avoidance that influence his ability to participate in everyday activities such as feeding,
grooming, and regulation. Therapeutic support should focus on increasing his tolerance to
sensory input, using movement and visual engagement as strengths, and promoting adaptive
strategies to help him feel secure and regulated throughout his day.
